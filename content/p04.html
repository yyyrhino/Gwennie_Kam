钝的倾听：“刺聆”之后

In Numbness We Listen: An Afterword to “Cacophony”


金佐宁

Gwendoline Cho-ning Kam

「有時候，我情願鈍一點」
—— 陳棟帆

「刺聆」，作為陳棟帆個展「怒火烈幻海」延伸的現場項目，它所回應的，是藝術家的「鈍」。

比起以往的創作，陳棟帆最新的非具象作品展現出更為清晰和堅定的形狀，點、線、面之間亦存在著更多象徵包容、能屈能伸的彎曲轉向 (turn)，柔軟而不失韌性。「在千島湖下欄塢隧道創作《彩虹隧道》的時候，耳機中的巴赫，總能及時地扭轉我內心那突如其來的，如烈焰一般的情緒」。聆聽，是陳棟帆在創作中獲取靈感的另一途徑，也是負性能量的阻尼器，觸發筆鋒拐彎的契機。在聲音的無形場域中，他一次又一次地以畫筆為武器，色彩為留痕，抒發自我邊界在生活的跌宕起伏中，不斷地受到挑戰而建立的捍衛機制—— 沉澱、修復、擴容，而後再又被衝擊、再平緩，周而復始。此時，繪畫中的身體在生活的虛實邊界處，隨聲音往復躍動，似在批判生活程式化的限制，也似在展示程式化中尚未人知的境地及可能性。

在文字越來越難接近真實的時刻，聲音對陳棟帆那微妙且深層抽象的影響，就如諸多聲音藝術事件一樣，是文字描述的未竟之地。假若在寧靜的展廳中放眼感受各種節奏的碰撞：樸鈍的筆鋒，流動的線條，對比強烈的色彩，被解構的程式化，虛擬與真實的重疊…它們都在悄悄地將畫廊空間推向於一個即興劇場的轉化，構建著一個以聲音和行為表演進入展覽的通道。由此，「刺聆」把聲音作為一種連接的紐帶和策略，以其詮釋運動，再以運動詮釋情緒，將京胡尖刺的聲響，旦角颯爽多變的身段，融入至「怒火烈幻海」的展覽之中。

京胡和旦角，無不讓人想到即將上演的是典型的京劇表現形式。然而，「刺聆」既沒有從表面選擇貼合展覽主題或在地敘事的《雷峰塔》，更沒有成套程式化的京劇表演。在人群的焦點之中，有的只是旦角李星星和琴師李筱婧，兩位身體語言曾受科班京劇知識編碼的表演者。她們與在場的眾人一樣，生活於同一社會時代背景，但成長上所受的傳統戲曲訓練，又使她們擁有了跨越時空的，另類面對世界和表達自我的方式。在「刺聆」現場，兩位表演者被邀請利用自身的感受，調動大腦和身體最直覺的反應，以運動詮釋並回應陳棟帆畫中的「怒」「火」「烈」「幻」「海」。在拋開戲曲慣性的程式化表演，拒絕學科領域的僵化劃分，「刺聆」試圖實踐的，是透過同一時空的情緒共鳴，以經驗的即興，來尋找當代與傳統間持續、有效且真誠對話的可能。

隨著一聲高亢的京胡劃過畫廊的建築主體，觀眾在聽覺的喚醒中進入至另一空間維度。當琴弓的馬尾毛快速來回摩擦胡琴上的鋼弦時，聞聲起舞的旦角激活了陳棟帆畫中早已蟄伏能量的點、線、面。舉手投足，一笑一顰，旦角和琴師在表演之中與藝術家的筆觸和色彩相互延伸，冲破著畫框所制定的物理界限。由平面到無形的立體，速度和力量讓觀眾以聲音和運動作為參照，從異於慣常觀看展覽的方式，再度身臨藝術家的思想和情緒場域：在焦躁、追風逐電和荊棘滿布的社會，我們該如何在各種挑戰中鍛煉自我，保持清醒，從而變得更能從容地掌握邊界的彈性和韌度？《夜深沉》的劍、《哪吒令》的槍、《小開門》的水袖、《梅花》的長綢，無論是聲音、身段或武器，它們從不同維度解構重組藝術家的意圖同時，也推展著另一種敘事——人生如戲真亦假，戲如人生假亦真。在展廳裡的平衡時空中穿梭，旦角表演者時而為畫中人，時而為其自身，也時而亦為導賞人。當她在表演中嘗試以武器對抗畫中所投射的生活棘刺時，虛實相交的刹那，我們或許會驚覺那武器只是一種道具，而端首的形狀，竟是鈍的。
鈍，並非是對世界的敷衍怠慢，而是一種責任和態度。生活在受資本脈衝牽引的年代，每晨率先進入人們眼帘的，也許不再是窗外的風景，而是手機屏幕上眼花繚亂的信息景觀。這種縮影了權力競爭與博弈的畫面，時刻刺激著社會運轉的節奏，也挑戰著人們價值觀和認知的邊界。利用加速時代所營造的「無暇」氛圍，信息系統甚至以快速的甩動來尋找利益的共謀伙伴，並謂之為「銳」者。陳棟帆意識到這樣的快速是矛盾且危險的，人們在其中的情緒表達容易被削弱而淪為無效，而邊界亦在渾然不覺間被消解。因此，他以「鈍」作為一種生活規則，一種創作和感知事物的方法，務求以最沉穩細膩的態度，真摯回饋這正在忘形衝刺的社會，一個喘息和思考的空間，並由此使得邊界在不斷的突破中有效擴容。為此，「鈍」是「頓」，也是「盾」，就如水袖和長綢一樣，收放自如，起伏皆宜。

“I’d rather be numb, sometimes.”
—— Chen Dongfan

"Cacophony," an adjunct live on-site program to Chen Dongfan's solo exhibition "Wrath Blaze Ferocity Mirage Abyss," is curated in response to the artist's performance of numbness, a conviction that has permeated his work in recent years.


In his most recent non-figurative paintings, Chen Dongfan demonstrates clearer and more identifiable forms compared to his earlier creations. Using dots, lines, and planes to form curves, Chen conveys a sense of tolerance, flexibility, softness, and resilience, alluding to his daily encounters and emotional swings.“My sudden, fiery feeling is always, almost instantaneously, resolved by Bach's music in the course of my creation. When I did the painting of the "Rainbow Tunnel" at Qiandao Lake, the same experience recurs.” Listening, as a counter-buffer to negative energies, is a conduit of inspiration that triggers turning points in Chen Dongfan's paintings. The invisible field of sound provides Chen with a space in which he brushes to trace the border-defending mechanism - a recurring process of subsiding, repairing, and expanding - that he establishes to counteract ups and downs. Paintbrushes, in other words, become Chen's imaginary weapon, both for advance and retreat in the face of life’s ever-unexpected challenges. As reality and imagination intersect in sound, the body in painting, also in musicking, performs as if criticizing the limitations of stylized life, yet unfolding the unknown realm of stylization.

The subtle, abstract influence of sound on Chen Dongfan's creation, just as many sound events, is an unfinished business of text, especially in an age when writing is increasingly difficult to approach the real. Standing in the quiet gallery of Inna Art Space, one can experience the collision of various visual rhythms: blunt strokes, flowing lines, contrasting colors… all of which transform the gallery into an improvisational theater where the presence of sound and movement is possible. “Cacophony” thus navigates sound, excluding verbal text, as a strategic link to bridge the ear-piercing jinghu (Chinese bowed fiddle) and the brave, heroic actions of the martial female role from Chinese jingju theater to the exhibition, serving as a translator of the artist’s inner thoughts.


Speaking of the jinghu and the martial female role, one could easily associate “Cacophony” with a traditional, programmatic mode of jingju theater performance. This live event, however, deliberately avoids any stereotypical representations of jingju or libretti like “Leifeng Pagoda” that superficially echo the exhibition theme with local narratives. “Cacophony” focuses on how body language encoded by professional jingju training could become a medium of representing the notions of “wrath,” “blaze,” “ferocity,” “mirage,” and “abyss” in Chen Dongfan’s work. The two performers, jingju martial female artist Li Xingxing and jinghu musician Li Xiaojing, are invited to perform with minds and bodies mobilized by their most intuitive reactions to the paintings on site. Equipped with alternative, traditional means to express themselves in the contemporary world, the two Li’s have created a channel that crosses time and space, bringing the audience to Chen’s creations via an atypical and unprecedented audiovisual experience.

When a piercing treble note of the jinghu sounds across the gallery’s architecture, the audience is spiritually transported into another spatial realm in an auditory awakening. Dancing along to the constant rubbing between the horsetail bow and the metal strings of jinghu, the martial female performer activates the energy-dormant dots, lines, and planes in the paintings with her charming actions and even the slightest facial expression. Beyond the physical confines of the frames, brushstrokes of paint are now extended into the gallery, performing interlocking extensions with the bodies of performers. In the power of speed and intensity, sound and movement work jointly to prompt the audience to ponder over Chen Dongfan’s thoughts in his works: In this anxious, wind-chasing world, how can we exercise ourselves in all kinds of challenges?


How can we comfortably stay tuned in adjusting the position and flexibility of our boundaries? While de- and re-constructing Chen’s ideas from multiple perspectives, jingju music excerpts (qupai) paired with symbolic weapons and costumes (sword, spear, water-sleeves, and long silk) in emotional correspondences have pushed the show toward another narrative: life is like a play, and always vice versa. Traveling through the parallel universes in the gallery, the martial female performer switches freely between being herself, a character in Chen’s works, and a docent of the exhibition. As she attempts to use her weapon to fight against the thorns of life projected in the paintings, the moment reality meets reality, it is surprising to realize that in her hands is only a prop whose head end is blunt and numb.


To remain numb does not mean to face the world with perfunctory negligence but with sincerity and responsibility. Living in an era driven by the pulse of capital, the dazzling information landscape on our cell phones, rather than that outside the windows, becomes the first thing coming into our eyes every morning. As an embodiment of competition and power play in society, such a digital landscape constantly stimulates the rhythm of social operation and challenges the boundaries of people's values and perceptions. Unfortunately, in this era of infomania, the light always shines on those complicit partners who are considered capable of catching up with the hurtling, accelerated system. Chen Dongfan realizes that such rapidity is paradoxically dangerous, as people's emotional expression is easily weakened and rendered ineffective, and boundaries risk being unwittingly dissolved. "To be numb" becomes Chen's rule of life and a way of creating and perceiving the world. In a humble will, he strives to reward this spinning society with a sanctuary where we can pause and breathe to ensure that every step we take to expand our boundaries is valid and effective in continuous breakthroughs. To this end, numbness calls for a break, a shelter from where we will be blessed with energies to deal freely with whatever challenges come our way.
