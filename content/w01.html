自由談


Noise on Noise


何為噪音？當我們將某一種聲音定義為噪音，又或將噪音僅與小範圍的聲音藝術形式緊密聯繫時，這些行為所暗示的，是一種即將失去的危機，還是一種挖掘可能的契機？作品《自由談》的標題取自於《申報》的同名副刊，延續了上個世紀對鑼鼓的關注和討論。影像中，即興戲劇「一字經」無聲地進行。藝術家就鑼鼓在當代聲音生態中的地位及其被視為「噪音」的境遇發起連串提問，而演員則以無音調的拼音組織回應。


作為作品中唯一被體現的聲音，程式化的鑼鼓聲憑其深植於中國文化的語言、音樂和圖像特質，與劇中的無聲文本形成了一種相互辯證的視聽「契約」關係。當即興劇本在不同演員的互動中接力流轉，最終所反映出的不僅是對文字和聲音的集體意識，更是在對消失近一個世紀的鑼鼓評論之重新發起中，再度審視今天所熱議的「噪音」意義。

What does it insinuate when we define a certain sound as noise, or when we associate noise only closely with a narrow range of sound art forms (such as electronic noise)? Is that a crisis of impending cultural loss, or an opportunity to explore possibilities?


Noise on Noise, with its Chinese title succeeding that of Ziyoutan (Free Forum), an eponymous supplement to the newspaper Shen Bao (Shanghai News) published last century, attempts to reactivate the long-disappeared social discussion of Chinese theatrical luogu (gongs and drums). In this audiovisual work, an improv theatre, "One Word Story," unfolds silently via written unpitched pronunciation, intersecting with a contextual sound delivered by luogu.


Possessing deep roots in everyday life throughout history, luogu has sustained the viability of traditional Chinese theatres for centuries and frequented the headlines of social criticism during the 1930s. The unfortunate loss of luogu commenting today reflects the wane of the once-valued auditory culture and social limitations in comprehending the so-called "noise." By answering sets of questions based on the present sound ecology of luogu, the performers present a collective consciousness toward cultural text and sound, while relaunching the commenting mechanism for the noise aesthetic in Chinese culture. Audiences are prompted to reconsider the meaning of noise despite its seeming popularity upheld by today’s contemporary art world.
